# Mac App Store camera requirements for ReplayMac — research report

**Date:** 2026-11-01 · **Scope:** macOS App Store (MAS) app that captures the webcam and composites it into screen recordings · **App Review Guidelines cited are the version last updated October 16, 2026** (https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i). All sources accessed 2026-11-01.

---

## 1. Entitlements — `com.apple.security.device.camera`

- **Yes, it is required for sandboxed MAS apps.** Apple's entitlement reference: `com.apple.security.device.camera` is "a Boolean value that indicates whether the app may interact with the built-in and external cameras, and capture movies and still images" (available on macOS since 10.7).
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/entitlements/com.apple.security.device.camera
- **How to get it:** enable the **App Sandbox** (or Hardened Runtime) capability in Xcode, then under *Hardware* select **Camera** ("To add this entitlement to your app, first enable the App Sandbox or Hardened Runtime capability in Xcode, and then select Camera" — same doc). For Mac App Store builds the entitlement is included in the provisioning profile automatically once the capability is on — **no special Apple approval / entitlement request** is needed (unlike e.g. CarPlay Audio, Hypervisor, or Privileged File Operations, which are limited-use entitlements per the Guidelines intro).
- MAS apps **must be sandboxed** — Guideline 2.4.5(i): "Apps distributed via the Mac App Store … must be appropriately sandboxed."
  https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#2.4.5
- **What happens without it:** the sandbox blocks camera access at the OS level regardless of TCC consent — "A TCC grant does not override App Sandbox restrictions. A sandboxed camera client needs `com.apple.security.device.camera`" (HackTricks TCC reference: https://hacktricks.wiki/en/macos-hardening/macos-security-and-privilege-escalation/macos-security-protections/macos-tcc/index.html). Symptom: `AVCaptureDevice.DiscoverySession` returns no devices / "No video capture devices were found", session fails to start with `NSOSStatusErrorDomain -54`, or you get black frames (Apple Developer Forums, camera tag, 2025). The entitlement doc also notes: "In macOS 10.14 and later, the user must explicitly grant permission for each app to access cameras" — i.e. entitlement **and** user consent are both required.
- **App Review note:** reviewers reject apps carrying entitlements with no matching functionality ("App rejected for entitlements the app needs", https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/847115) — so only add the camera entitlement if the binary actually uses the camera, and mention camera use in App Review notes.

## 2. Info.plist — `NSCameraUseUsageDescription`

- **Required on macOS since 10.14 (Mojave).** Apple: "In macOS 10.14 and later, the user must explicitly grant permission for each app to access cameras and microphones… If your app uses device cameras, include the `NSCameraUsageDescription` key in your app's Info.plist." And, critically: **"If the appropriate key is not present in your app's Info.plist when your app requests authorization or attempts to use a capture device, the system terminates your app."** (crash, with a console message explaining why).
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/requesting-authorization-for-media-capture-on-macos
  Related: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/requesting-authorization-to-capture-and-save-media ("In iOS and macOS 10.14 and later, the user must explicitly grant permission for each app to access the camera and microphone.")
- **Wording requirements (Guideline 5.1.1(ii), current text):** "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection… Ensure your purpose strings clearly and completely describe your use of the data." App Review looks for purpose strings that **explain specifically how the app will use the camera and give a concrete example** — insufficient/vague purpose strings are a common 5.1.1 rejection ("Write clear purpose strings", Tech Talks 110152: https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/tech-talks/110152/). HIG style rules: brief, complete sentence; sentence case; active voice; end with a period (e.g. "ReplayMac records your camera into your screen recordings when you turn on the webcam overlay."). HIG Privacy: https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy
- **Just-in-time vs at launch:** request permission **only when the feature is first used**, not at launch. HIG: "Request permission only when your app clearly needs access to the data or resource… Ideally, wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature that requires access… Avoid requesting permission at launch unless the data or resource is required for your app to function." Apple's media-capture doc says the same for camera specifically: "Call `requestAccess` before starting capture, but only at a time that's appropriate for your app. For example, if photo or video recording isn't the main focus of your app, check for camera permission only when the user invokes your app's camera-related features." (URL above). Backed by Guidelines 5.1.1(iii) data minimization and 5.1.1(iv) ("Apps must respect the user's permission settings and not attempt to manipulate, trick, or force people to consent to unnecessary data access").

## 3. App Store Review Guidelines relevant to camera (text as of the Oct 16, 2026 revision)

Source: https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/ (last updated October 16, 2026)

- **2.5.14 — the "recording users" rule (key one):** "Apps must request explicit user consent and provide a clear visual and/or audible indication when recording, logging, or otherwise making a record of user activity. **This includes any use of the device camera, microphone, screen recordings, or other user inputs.**" → ReplayMac must show its own obvious in-app "recording" UI (not rely only on the OS indicator) and must have consent.
- **5.1.1 Data Collection and Storage:** (i) privacy policy link required in App Store Connect metadata **and** in-app; (ii) consent + clear purpose strings (see §2); (iii) data minimization — only request access relevant to core functionality; (iv) respect permission settings, no manipulation/tricks, offer alternatives for users who decline; (vi) surreptitious discovery of private data → removal from the Developer Program; (viii) no compiling personal information from any source without the user's explicit consent.
- **5.1.2 Data Use and Sharing:** (i) "you may not use, transmit, or share someone's personal data without first obtaining their permission" and must disclose where data is shared; (ii) no repurposing without further consent; **(vi) "Data gathered from … depth and facial mapping tools (such as ARKit, Camera APIs, or Photo APIs) may not be used for marketing, advertising or use-based data mining, including by third parties."** → camera frames must never be sent to analytics/ad networks/AI training etc.
- **2.3 Accurate Metadata:** "all your app metadata, **including privacy information**, your app description, screenshots, and previews accurately reflect the app's core experience." Note: **2.3.10 is not camera-related** — it prohibits referencing other mobile platforms/alternative marketplaces in metadata. The camera-relevant metadata rule is 2.3 overall.
- Also relevant: **1.6 Data Security** (appropriate security for user information), **2.4.5** (MAS sandbox rules), **2.5.1** (public APIs only; must run on the currently shipping OS), **2.5.19** (no hidden/dormant/undocumented features — new as of Oct 16, 2026; a webcam overlay must be visible/documented, not hidden).
- **Camera-in-use indicator:** macOS shows a menu-bar indicator (camera dot in Control Center) whenever any process uses the camera, and since macOS 15.1 a permanent screen-recording indicator while the screen is captured. **There is no API for an app to suppress these indicators, and the camera hardware LED cannot be software-disabled.** The only user-controllable option is hiding indicators on *external displays* in full screen (macOS Sonoma 14.4+): https://support.apple.com/en-us/118449. Apps must not attempt to hide indicators from their own recordings — combined with 2.5.14, the app should show its own clear recording indication while the OS indicator is on. (Whether the menu-bar dot appears inside a given screen recording depends on what the capture includes; with ScreenCaptureKit you can exclude the menu bar via `SCContentFilter` if you want.)

## 4. Privacy manifest (`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`) and nutrition label

- **Manifest required since May 1, 2024** for **all new apps and app updates** on all platforms incl. macOS: the app target must ship a `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`, and every third-party SDK must carry its own manifest (an app's manifest cannot cover its SDKs). Announcement: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=3d8a9yyh (Feb 29, 2024: "Starting May 1: You'll need to include approved reasons for the listed APIs used by your app's code to upload a new or updated app"). Docs: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy-manifest-files and https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/adding-a-privacy-manifest-to-your-app-or-third-party-sdk
- **Third-party SDKs:** since **February 12, 2025**, apps using SDKs on Apple's "commonly used third-party SDKs" list must use SDK versions that include a valid privacy manifest, and SDKs added as **binary dependencies** on that list must also be **signed** (list: https://developer.apple.com/support/third-party-SDK-requirements/ ; "SDKs that require a privacy manifest and signature" in Upcoming Requirements; news: https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=av1nevon via https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/).
- **Camera is NOT a required-reason API.** The required-reason API list (`NSPrivacyAccessedAPITypes`) still consists of exactly five categories as of 2026: **FileTimestamp, SystemBootTime, DiskSpace, ActiveKeyboards, UserDefaults** — `AVCaptureDevice`/`AVCaptureSession` and other camera APIs are **not** on it, so no required-reason declaration is needed for camera access itself.
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/privacy_manifest_files/describing_use_of_required_reason_api
  (You will still need e.g. `CA92.1` for UserDefaults, `C617.1`/`3B52.1` for file timestamps of recordings, `E174.1` if you check free disk space before recording.)
- **Nutrition label / `NSPrivacyCollectedDataTypes`:** Apple's definition — "'Collect' refers to transmitting data off the device in a way that allows you and/or your third-party partners to access it for a period longer than what is necessary to service the transmitted request in real time." And explicitly: **"You use location, device identifiers, and other sensitive data, but only on device, and the data is never sent to a server → Data that is processed only on device is not 'collected' and does not need to be disclosed."**
  https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/
  → **Camera video composited into recordings stored only in local files and never transmitted does NOT have to be declared** on the App Store privacy label. If any camera frames/video/audio ever leave the device (share/upload, cloud sync, crash reports, telemetry), declare the **"Photos or Videos"** data type ("The user's photos or videos") and **"Audio Data"** for mic, with purpose "App Functionality" and "not linked to user identity" only if truly de-identified; note "data collected on an ongoing basis after an initial request for permission must be disclosed."
- **What similar MAS apps disclose:** screen-recorder apps on the Mac App Store typically declare **"The developer does not collect any data from this app"** (e.g. Capzo: Screenshot & Recorder, https://apps.apple.com/ci/app/capzo-screenshot-recorder/id6788553417), consistent with the on-device-only rule; Screen Studio (direct-distributed) likewise markets that "recordings are stored and processed locally" (https://screen.studio/create/screen-recorder-with-audio). Either way, you must still answer the privacy questions in App Store Connect for every submission, keep them accurate (Guideline 2.3), and link a privacy policy (5.1.1(i)).

## 5. TCC on macOS for camera in a sandboxed MAS app

- Flow (macOS 10.14+): check `AVCaptureDevice.authorizationStatus(for: .video)` (macOS 10.14+); if `.notDetermined`, call `AVCaptureDevice.requestAccess(for: .video)` — macOS shows the system alert with your `NSCameraUseUsageDescription`; the system **remembers** the choice; the user can change it in **System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera**. If **denied**: "video recordings contain only black frames" (audio: silence).
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/authorizationstatus(for:)
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/bundleresources/requesting-authorization-for-media-capture-on-macos
- **Where consent is stored:** the TCC databases — system-wide `/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/TCC.db` and per-user `~/Library/Application Support/com.apple.TCC/TCC.db`, managed by `tccd` (https://hacktricks.wiki/.../macos-tcc/index.html ; https://spektion.com/articles/tampering-with-macos-tcc). The App Sandbox does **not** change where consent is stored or how prompting works — it adds a separate, static gate: the entitlement. So for a sandboxed MAS app, **`com.apple.security.device.camera` + the normal TCC camera grant is sufficient; nothing else is needed**. MAS apps appear in the Camera privacy list by bundle ID, and consent persists across app updates (TCC entries are keyed to bundle ID/code signature).
- **Known issues:** Apple Developer Forums (2025) report that in sandboxed apps **Continuity Camera (iPhone as webcam) can be absent from `AVCaptureDevice` lists** and System Information shows "No video capture devices were found" — built-in and USB cameras work with the entitlement, but test Continuity Camera and external cameras on your target OS. AVCaptureSession itself is fully supported in the MAS sandbox with the entitlement.
- **Polling is safe:** calling `authorizationStatus(for:)` repeatedly **never triggers a prompt** — only `requestAccess` when status is `.notDetermined` does. After the user denies, `requestAccess` will not re-prompt; guide the user to System Settings instead (deep link that only *opens* settings: `x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Camera`).

## 6. Efficient camera capture + compositing on macOS (Apple guidance)

- `AVCaptureSession` + `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput` is the standard, sandbox-compatible path (VideoDataOutput available macOS 10.7+). https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturevideodataoutput
- **Pixel format — important correction to the old "always 32BGRA" advice:** TN3121 "Selecting a pixel format for an AVCaptureVideoDataOutput" (still current): **avoid defaulting to `kCVPixelFormatType_32BGRA`** — it is not a native capture format, so AVCapture must convert every frame, and BGRA buffers use ~2.6× the memory/bandwidth of `420v`. Query `availableVideoPixelFormatTypes` and prefer native bi-planar YCbCr (`420v`/`420f`, lossy variants if supported) if your pipeline can consume YUV (Metal shaders can, and `AVAssetWriter` accepts bi-planar YCbCr directly); use BGRA only if the consumer requires RGBA (e.g. an ML model). https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3121-selecting-a-pixel-format-for-an-avcapturevideodataoutput
- **Realtime settings:** `alwaysDiscardsLateVideoFrames = true` (drops late frames instead of queueing); `setSampleBufferDelegate(_:queue:)` — "You must use a **serial dispatch queue**, to guarantee that video frames will be delivered in order"; if your processing can't keep up, Apple suggests adjusting min frame duration rather than relying on frame drops (note `minFrameDuration` on the output is deprecated in current docs — use `AVCaptureConnection.videoMinFrameDuration` / `AVCaptureDevice.activeVideoMinFrameDuration`).
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturevideodataoutput/setsamplebufferdelegate(_:queue:)
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturevideodataoutput/alwaysdiscardslatevideoframes
- **Session preset:** pick the lowest `AVCaptureSession.Preset` that fits the overlay (webcam in a corner only needs e.g. `.hd1280x720` or less) to save CPU/GPU/thermals. https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturesession/preset-swift.enum
- **Compositing:** get the `CVPixelBuffer` from the `CMSampleBuffer` and wrap it in `CIImage(cvPixelBuffer:)`; render with a Metal-backed `CIContext` (`render(_:to:)` into a `CVPixelBuffer` or `render(_:to:commandBuffer:...)` into an `MTLTexture`), or go zero-copy with `CVMetalTextureCacheCreateTextureFromImage` to turn camera/screen `CVPixelBuffer`s into `MTLTexture`s for a single Metal composite pass, then encode with `AVAssetWriter`. ScreenCaptureKit `SCStream` frames are also `CVPixelBuffer`s, so camera + screen composite in one Metal pass. Apple pipeline example: WWDC22 "Display HDR video in EDR with AVFoundation and Metal" https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2022/110565 ; CIContext Metal render: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coreimage/cicontext/render(_:to:commandbuffer:bounds:colorspace:)
- **Device enumeration/persistence:** `AVCaptureDevice.DiscoverySession(deviceTypes: [.builtInWideAngleCamera, .external], mediaType: .video, position: .unspecified)` — `devices` is KVO-observable for hot-plug; persist `AVCaptureDevice.uniqueID` to remember the user's chosen camera — "persists on one system across device connections and disconnections, application restarts, and reboots."
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/discoverysession
  https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avcapturedevice/uniqueid

## 7. macOS 15 / 26 / 27 (2025–2026) specifics

- **Naming:** macOS 26 "Tahoe" shipped Sept 15, 2025 (Xcode 26); **macOS 27 "Golden Gate" shipped September 14, 2026** and is now the *currently shipping OS* — Guideline 2.5.1 requires apps to run on it, so test on macOS 27 even with a macOS 15 deployment target. (https://support.apple.com/en-us/127255 ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacOS_Golden_Gate)
- No new camera entitlement or privacy-manifest category was introduced in macOS 15/26 — the required-reason API list is still the original five (see §4), and camera APIs remain outside it.
- **Screen-recording side (since ReplayMac also records the screen):** macOS 15.0 briefly re-prompted weekly for Screen Recording permission; from 15.1 it's monthly; **macOS 15.1+ shows a permanent menu-bar recording indicator** while any app captures the screen; macOS Tahoe requires a proper app bundle for Screen Recording permission and Apple recommends ScreenCaptureKit over legacy `CGDisplayStream`. (TidBITS on the Sequoia prompts: https://tidbits.com/2024/08/19/apple-reduces-excessive-sequoia-permission-requests-shifts-to-monthly/ ; ScreenCaptureKit: https://developer.apple.com/documentation/screencapturekit)
- **Privacy indicators:** since macOS Sonoma 14.4 users can hide camera/mic indicators on external displays in full screen (https://support.apple.com/en-us/118449); on macOS 26 the recording indicator is shown blue in the menu bar — none of these can be suppressed by apps.
- **Notarization:** not required for Mac App Store distribution — MAS submission includes Apple's own signing/review; notarization applies only to Developer ID distribution outside the store. https://developer.apple.com/macos/distribution/
- **Guidelines current version:** last updated **October 16, 2026** (https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=92th7e5i), which notably added 2.5.19 (no hidden/undocumented features). "Tracking" definition unchanged: linking app-collected data with third-party data for ads/ad-measurement or sharing with a data broker; **on-device-only processing is not tracking** (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/).

## 8. Just-in-time use and permission-state polling

- **Request camera permission only when the user first uses a camera feature** (Apple media-capture doc note + HIG, see §2); don't ask at launch. Correspondingly, **don't run the camera while not recording**: start the `AVCaptureSession` when recording with webcam starts, stop it when recording stops — this satisfies 2.5.14 (clear indication while recording), 5.1.1(iii) minimization and 5.1.1(iv), and it also turns off the OS camera-in-use indicator when idle.
- **Polling `AVCaptureDevice.authorizationStatus(for: .video)` never triggers a prompt** — safe to call anytime (e.g. to update UI). Only `requestAccess(for:)` when `.notDetermined` shows the system alert, once; after denial it won't re-prompt — deep-link System Settings (`x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_Camera`) as the fallback.

## Action items for ReplayMac

1. **Entitlement:** enable App Sandbox capability → Hardware → **Camera** (`com.apple.security.device.camera`) in Xcode; verify it's in the MAS provisioning profile and in `codesign -d --entitlements` output of the release build. Don't add unused entitlements (App Review checks).
2. **Info.plist:** add `NSCameraUseUsageDescription` with a specific, active-voice sentence, e.g. "ReplayMac records your camera and adds it as an overlay to your screen recordings when you enable the webcam." (App crashes on macOS 10.14+ without this key when the camera is touched.) If mic is used, add `NSMicrophoneUsageDescription` too. If you record *system audio*, add `NSAudioCaptureUsageDescription`.
3. **Permission flow:** check `authorizationStatus(for: .video)` first; call `requestAccess` **only when the user first enables the webcam/recording feature** (just-in-time, not at launch); handle `.denied` by deep-linking System Settings > Privacy & Security > Camera. Never poll `requestAccess` in a loop.
4. **Recording indication (Guideline 2.5.14):** show a clear in-app visual/audible "recording" state whenever the camera or screen is being recorded; get explicit user consent; don't hide the OS camera indicator from recordings; don't keep the session running when not recording.
5. **Privacy manifest:** ship `PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy` for the app target (declare UserDefaults `CA92.1`, file-timestamp `C617.1`/`3B52.1`, disk-space `E174.1` as applicable — **no camera required-reason entry needed**); ensure every SPM/binary third-party SDK has its own manifest (and signature if it's on Apple's list). Generate Xcode's privacy report and review it before each upload.
6. **Nutrition label (App Store Connect):** if all camera video stays on-device, answer "does not collect data" per Apple's on-device-only rule; if any frames/audio ever leave the device (share, upload, crash reports), declare **Photos or Videos** (+ **Audio Data**) with purpose "App Functionality". Keep metadata/privacy answers accurate (2.3) and link a privacy policy in ASC metadata and in-app (5.1.1(i)). Never use camera data for ads/analytics/data mining (5.1.2(vi)).
7. **TCC/sandbox:** expect the standard system prompt; consent persists per bundle ID; test built-in camera, USB camera, and Continuity Camera on macOS 15 **and** macOS 27 (currently shipping OS, Guideline 2.5.1).
8. **Performance:** use `AVCaptureVideoDataOutput` with a **serial delegate queue**, `alwaysDiscardsLateVideoFrames = true`, a low session preset (e.g. `.hd1280x720`), and a native YUV pixel format (`420v`/`420f` via `availableVideoPixelFormatTypes`) rather than defaulting to 32BGRA (TN3121); composite camera + ScreenCaptureKit frames as `CVPixelBuffer`→`MTLTexture` (CVMetalTextureCache) or `CIImage`/`CIContext` in one Metal pass; encode via `AVAssetWriter`. Persist the chosen camera with `AVCaptureDevice.uniqueID`; enumerate with `DiscoverySession(deviceTypes: [.builtInWideAngleCamera, .external])`.
9. **Screen recording side:** use ScreenCaptureKit; expect the macOS 15.1+/26 menu-bar recording indicator and the periodic (monthly) screen-recording re-prompt on macOS 15+; MAS apps need no separate notarization.
10. **Before submitting:** re-read the current guidelines (updated Oct 16, 2026) at https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/, describe the webcam feature specifically in App Review notes (2.3.1(a)), and verify no hidden camera behavior (2.5.19).
